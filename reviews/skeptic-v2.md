# Credit Skeptic Review v2: content.js (2026-10-06)

## 1. Five strongest objections (ranked)

**1. The Meta pair contradicts the thesis.** Page 2 says "pricing tracks the lease counterparty more than the asset." But El Paso priced at T+287.5 while its twin, Hyperion, was "near T+230" in secondary. That is the same tenant, the same template and the same rating, roughly 60bps apart. The gap is supply or new issue concession, not tenant. Separately, a 5bp spread move cannot produce "a loss of close to ten points". The loss is almost entirely rates, which undercuts "charging more". **Fix:** say this outright, and have the fact checker reconcile the T+230 and low 90s figures (the implied Treasury move looks large).

**2. Beacon Point breaks the rating logic.** It is a Baa2 bond at 95% LTC and it priced at T+165, tighter than A+ El Paso. Without tenor or weighted average life this cannot be read at all. If Beacon Point is shorter, the comparison is a curve effect. **Fix:** add a tenor or WAL column to the table, and state that the market prices the tenant while the agencies notch for structure.

**3. "The 325bps gap ... is the market's price for counterparty risk."** DDTL 4.0 (March) and 5.5 (August) differ in date, rating, contract tenor, advance rate and collateral pool. Your own "100bps wider than its May loan at the same ratings" shows that roughly a third of the gap is timing. **Fix:** change to "reflects counterparty, contract length and market timing", and give DDTL 3.0 a date.

**4. "Leverage by Financing Route" mixes measures.** It puts LTC (construction cost), appraisal LTV (Aligned), KBRA haircut LTV (108.4%) and a GPU advance rate (65%) on one axis. These are not comparable. 108% deal LTV says nothing about the A- or AAA classes without tranche attachment points. **Fix:** label each bar by its measure, or chart only LTC.

**5. Oracle lost its balance.** v1 had Oracle's "on schedule" response and S&P's 4.5x downgrade trigger. Both are gone. "As banks struggled to sell it down" is causal, and 89 to 91 is a quote, not a trade. **Fix:** restore both items and write "was quoted at 89 to 91 during syndication."

## 2. Overstated claims
- "the highest loan to cost on record for a data center bond" is an unsourced superlative. Cut it.
- "one obligation is a lease to one agency and debt to another." Moody's says it will treat *part* of the leases as debt. Soften to match.
- "S&P accepted Hyperion as off balance sheet" needs a citation.
- "could let it defer rent for up to three years". Per fact-check R2 this applies only if the claim is accepted, and rent has not yet started.
- "In every route the lender is underwriting the tenant." That is false for GPU loans, where the advance rate depends on collateral value.

## 3. Missing pieces
- **DSCR and the lease tail.** Lease-backed debt is sized on contracted rent coverage and on the balloon left at lease expiry, not on LTC. This is the core mechanic, and it is absent.
- **The size of the RVG cap** relative to the outstanding bonds in year four.
- **Lease protections.** Hell or high water terms, rent abatement under force majeure (Jupiter shows they matter), and debt service reserves.
- **Recovery** on a stranded single tenant campus, and GPU residual values against the amortization schedule.
- **Aggregate OpenAI exposure** across the table, with a dollar total.
- **Holders.** Banks hold to maturity, while bond funds take marks like Hyperion's.

## 4. Confounds
- Pricing runs from October 2025 to September 2026 with no adjustment for index level.
- The table mixes fixed yields, T spreads, SOFR margins and dollar prices.
- Amortizing bonds are quoted against the 10 year, not against a benchmark matched to their WAL.
- Agencies are mixed in a single rating column.
- Digital Drive's "270bps" is a yield gap to an index, not a spread.
- Meta's 2066 spreads are compared across dates with different books.
- Fact checker should verify: the Amazon concession of "10 to 12bps against 4bps" (v1 had 12 against 2.25), the Abilene $9.6bn (R2 had a $2.3bn JPMorgan loan), the $662bn being "113% of adjusted debt", and "above 8% for the first time".

## 5. Depth of mechanics
The draft is better but not yet credit grade. It shows *that* leverage varies but not *why* a 95% LTC deal can be investment grade. The answer is that rent coverage and the lease tail bind, not cost. It never splits a spread into base rate, credit, structure and illiquidity, and concession. It does not explain why KBRA haircuts appraisals. One paragraph on DSCR and the tail, plus a WAL column, would close most of the gap.

## 6. Genuinely strong
- **The deal table.** It flags the derived Saline LTC and the guidance-only pricing honestly. Keep it.
- **The CoreWeave curve.** It holds the borrower constant and varies the payer. Keep it, with the caveat.
- **The KBRA 27.2% haircut and the Moody's versus S&P lease treatment.** These are real divergences of the kind a credit audience values.

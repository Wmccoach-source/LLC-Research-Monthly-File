# Credit Skeptic Review: content.js (2026-10-06)

## 1. Five strongest objections (ranked)

**1. Yields compared across dates and used as evidence of a risk premium.** "The higher cost on the same rating and the same tenant shows investors now charge for structure, supply, and duration." Hyperion priced in October 2025 and El Paso in July 2026, and Treasuries moved in between. A yield gap is not a spread gap. Page 1 blames project risk and page 2 blames structure, supply and duration. **Fix:** compare spreads at issue (add Hyperion's spread next to El Paso's 287.5bps), pick one explanation, and change "shows" to "suggests."

**2. The lease is called long, then four years.** The callout says "behind a long lease to Meta," but the risk paragraph says Hyperion's "lease has a four year initial term backed by a residual value guarantee." For a 2049 bond, that is the credit. **Fix:** cut "long." State that after year four, repayment rests on renewal and the RVG, and say who stands behind the RVG.

**3. Yield moves are not separated from rate moves.** "78 traded at higher yields on July 28 than at issuance, with a median increase of about 22bps." If Treasuries rose, nearly every bond shows this. **Fix:** restate as spread change, or benchmark against the IG index over the same window.

**4. CoreWeave is apples to oranges.** "$1.1bn of five year notes at 9.25%, roughly 270bps above similarly rated debt." No rating is named, the tenor is five years against 2048, and the tenant is high yield. Charting it beside A+ Meta paper implies one curve. **Fix:** name the rating and label it as a non-investment-grade tenant point.

**5. The maturity mismatch argument targets the wrong collateral.** "bonds reaching 2048 and 2049 against GPUs that last three to five years." The SPVs finance buildings and leases, not GPUs. **Fix:** show that GPUs are in the collateral, or rewrite it as lease term (four years) against bond term.

## 2. Overstated claims
- "It remains the largest private credit transaction ever." This is unsourced, and Hyperion was a 144A bond, so it may not count as private credit. Source it or cut it.
- "keeps leverage off the tenant's balance sheet." Agencies can impute the lease and RVG as debt, which the Moody's line concedes. Say "reported balance sheet."
- "borrowing directly puts the hyperscaler itself on the credit watchlist." This rests on one name. Amazon, Alphabet and Meta also borrow directly.
- "a distinct credit sector with its own spread behavior." The draft has no spread series behind it. Frame it as a hypothesis.

## 3. Missing pieces
- **OpenAI concentration** behind Oracle's backlog and CoreWeave's revenue. This is the biggest omission.
- Covenants, debt service reserves, and whether rent continues under force majeure.
- Recovery value of a single tenant campus.
- Who holds the paper, and whether the ratings are private.
- Capex of $750bn sits below OCF of $778bn, yet debt covers "about one third." Explain the gap (buybacks, dividends) or readers will see a contradiction.

## 4. Confounds and apples to oranges
- The issuer set shifts from four names ($194bn) to the "five largest hyperscalers" ($250bn).
- The dates are mixed: July 7, July 28, August and September.
- No agency is named for either SPV's A+, while Oracle's BBB- is S&P's.
- The 6.58% versus "roughly 5.5%" Meta corporate comparison has no matched tenor or date.
- The Jupiter lease terms rest on one anonymous source. Keep the attribution and don't lean on it.

## 5. What is genuinely strong
- The Hyperion and El Paso pair is a near natural experiment, with the same tenant and rating. Keep it, restated in spreads.
- The 91 bond sample with medians and the concession widening from 2.25bps to 12bps.
- The Oracle callout's balance. It includes the 4.5x downgrade trigger and Oracle's response. Do not trim it.

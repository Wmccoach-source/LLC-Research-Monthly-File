## Findings

1. Structure. Draft: page1 (p5), page2 (p6), page3 (p7). August: p5 Overview (explainer, market context, how it works graphic), p6 Market Activity plus a credit deterioration section with chart, p7 second topic with Overview, Market Growth, two side by side callouts, chart. Draft follows the cover, Overview, Market Activity, Risk Considerations, two callouts pattern, and it ends each page on a divergence line like August p6 and p7. Gaps to know about. (a) Draft p5 has an explainer but no true market context paragraph with headline market stats like August p5 ("YTD issuance reached $322.9bn, up 5.9% YoY"); p3 on page 5 is a credit argument instead. (b) Draft p6 has a table where August p6 has a chart, and the draft p6 has no chart slot. (c) Draft p7 carries the charts and callouts, which matches August p7. Fix: optionally add one aggregate sizing sentence to the draft p5 (for example total 2026 hyperscaler bond issuance, already planned in chart2) and confirm the p6 table fits where August used a chart.

2. Voice and density. Draft p5 p1 is about 13 lines, p2 and p3 are dense; August p5 and p6 paragraphs run 8 to 10 lines with 3 to 6 stats each. Draft is heavier on named deals and basis points, which matches the "dense, stat heavy" house rule but runs about 30 to 40 percent denser than August (August p6 Market Activity, p7 callouts). Draft also uses more first principles argument ("In every route the lender is underwriting the tenant"). Fix: trim p5 p1 by one or two sentences (the five routes can be shortened) and keep the closing divergence sentences, which match August p6 ("The key credit divergence in 2026 is between...").

3. Stale or time cutoff check. Draft has no "through July 7" style cutoff. Latest dated items are September 23, 24, and 10 and "late September", so it reads current for October. Risk: August p7 says data center ABS YTD "through July 6" and August p6 says "through August 2026"; the draft's Aligned ABS (July 2026, $1.18bn) post dates August's July 6 cutoff, so no conflict, but do not restate August's $3.3bn YTD figure as current.

4. Overlap with August data center ABS section (August p7, Data Center Growth). Draft p5 p1 and p2 and table mention Aligned $1.18bn ABS at 70% LTV and BX 2026 VLT10 CMBS. August p7 says S&P rated $9.3bn data center ABS in 2025, up from $3.9bn in 2024, and $3.3bn YTD through July 6. No factual conflict. Fix: add a short bridge so the case study reads as the next step from August, for example that stabilized campus ABS is the smallest of the five routes in dollar terms against the bond and loan routes. Do not repeat August's definitions of ABS, SPV, or tranche waterfall (August p5 and p6 already cover them); the draft correctly does not.

5. Macro and rate facts. August p2 and p5 (recap) give a hawkish Fed (rates 3.5% to 3.8%, September hike odds above 60%, 10 year through 4.8%, 2 year 4.4%), PCE 3.7%, HY spreads 260bps, BB 146bps, CCC 850bps. The draft carries no Fed, oil, or HY index facts, so nothing conflicts. However the draft's central claim that investors "charge more" (El Paso T+287.5, Oracle long bonds above 8%) sits against August's tight index spreads (HY 260bps, BB at a 52 week low). Fix: frame as AI tenant specific repricing against a tight index, a divergence consistent with August p3 ("split that continues to pull the index in two directions"). Whichever way the October recap reports September spreads and the Fed, the chair should check the draft's wording once the recap exists; this draft has no matching September numbers to verify against.

6. Named firms. Blue Owl appears in the draft (Hyperion 80% owner, Jupiter developer unit, Virginia campus for CoreWeave). Blue Owl is not named in the August PDF, so no conflict. Oracle, Apollo, and CoreWeave are also absent from August. August mentions Apollo (p4: Atlantic Aviation, nearly $10bn). Draft uses Apollo for the $3.5bn xAI chip vehicle. Low risk but if the recap names Apollo, keep roles distinct.

(c) El Paso rating "A+ / AA-" in table, explained only in callout; add a table note.

8. Contradictions inside the August report that this case study could inherit (list only, not fixed).
   - Brent crude. August p4 (Aug recap page 1 of report, L|L|C 1) says prices rose "back above $85/barrel"; August p4 (Macro Trends, L|L|C 3 page) says "back above $90". Draft does not cite oil. Avoid oil figures unless sourced to the October recap.
   - Data center ABS size. August p7 says outstanding data center issuance "surged from $4bn in 2020 to $61bn YTD in 2026, making up roughly 12% of the esoteric market", but the same page says the esoteric market is $210.9bn outstanding (12% would be about $25bn) and that YTD issuance through July 6 is $3.3bn. The $61bn, $25bn, and $3.3bn figures are not reconcilable. Do not use August's $61bn.
   - ABS issuance. August p5 says YTD ABS issuance through July is $322.9bn, while p7 says 2024's record was $338bn, with auto at 50.1% of 2026 (p6); not contradictory but easy to misquote as a full year comparison.
   - Aircraft. August p7 says $3.9bn outstanding across 16 deals yet industry issuance projected at $10 to $15bn for 2026; unclear definitions. Not relevant to the draft.
   - Fed rate path. August p4 says weak payrolls (down 23,000) initially fueled cut expectations, then hike odds above 60%; August p1 says 2 year yield jumped 12bps "largest since March". Any rate sentence in the draft should be checked against the October recap.
   - Typos in August (p5 and p6 duplicated phrase "influence both the supply and performance", "weaking", "grater"); not inherited, but do not copy phrasing.

# Fact Check: AI Infrastructure Financing case study

Reviewed 2026-10-06 by the fact-checker seat. Files: content.js, data/chart_data.json, chart rows in charts.js (A to D in full, plus the items in C, F and G that the draft relies on).

## Summary

I checked 69 items. **47 are confirmed, 5 conflict and 17 are unverifiable.** The draft mostly holds up. The Reuters and LSEG issuance statistics, the Goldman issuance and capex forecasts, the Hyperion structure (from Meta's own press release), the S&P Oracle downgrade and the Project Jupiter loan pricing all match sources. Five items are wrong or mis-framed. (1) The El Paso order book was about $20bn, or 1.6 times the deal, not $17bn. (2) Morgan Stanley's figure is an "$800bn+" private credit opportunity for data centers, not "up to $800bn". (3) The Reuters source on Jupiter said Oracle is not seeking to terminate the lease, which is different from saying it cannot. (4) Page 5 says Oracle borrows directly on its own balance sheet, but the page 6 Jupiter callout describes a project level, developer financed lease, so the draft contradicts itself. (5) "The same rating" for El Paso holds only at S&P, because Fitch rated El Paso AA-(EXP). Several values that feed the charts are unverifiable. These are the $778bn operating cash flow (chart C), the GPU 5 year upper bound (chart D), and three of the five yields in chart A: the exact 7.534%, Oracle's 10 year at 6.5% and CoreWeave's 9.25%, each of which appears only in aggregators. **On this pass only chart B has every value confirmed. Charts A, C and D must stay verified false.**

**Method caveat.** WebFetch was blocked by the network egress proxy for every domain I tried, including reuters.com, sec.gov, finance.yahoo.com, capacityglobal.com, theasset.com and bitget.com. Every verdict below therefore rests on WebSearch result text that attributes the figure to a named outlet or primary document. I could not open the pages themselves. "Confirmed" means the figure appeared consistently in press or primary results with a named original (a Reuters wire, Bloomberg, FT, Meta, S&P, BIS or Moody's). Before anything flips to verified true, a human should click through the source URL for each chart value. That should take under a minute per row.

## Most serious issues

1. **El Paso orders (id 31).** The draft says roughly $17bn of orders. Bloomberg derived reports and Zhitong both put orders near $20bn, about 1.6 times the deal size. Change to "drew roughly $20bn of orders, about 1.6 times the deal."
2. **Chart C operating cash flow of $778bn (id 61).** No source turns up for this figure. Goldman's own note, as quoted, says consensus capex of $755bn will reach about 100% of cash flow from operations. That implies operating cash flow near $755bn, which is in tension with $778bn and with the draft's framing of capex "against" a larger cash flow. Cut the $778bn number, or replace it with Goldman's "capex near 100% of operating cash flow" once the Goldman note is opened.
3. **Morgan Stanley figure (id 19).** Morgan Stanley Research ("Bridging the Data Center Gap") describes an "$800bn+" growth opportunity for private credit in data center financing through 2028, led by asset based finance. "Up to" reverses the direction. Suggested fix: "Morgan Stanley estimates private credit could supply more than $800bn of data center financing through 2028, led by asset based finance."
4. **Oracle lease wording (id 49).** Reuters reported that Oracle is not seeking to terminate the lease. The notice could let it defer rent by up to three years if the power commitments are missed, and it would still pay other costs and the full lease term. Change "cannot terminate the lease and remains responsible for debt costs" to "is not seeking to terminate the lease and would still owe rent for the full term".
5. **Oracle "borrowing directly" (id 8).** This contradicts the Jupiter callout on page 6, where about $18bn of project loans sit with a Blue Owl developer and Oracle is the tenant. Suggested fix: "Oracle has leaned more heavily on its own balance sheet, raising about $43bn of notes in fiscal 2026, while also leasing campuses financed at the project level."
6. **Yield comparisons (ids 22, 30).** Hyperion priced at about 225bps over Treasuries in October 2025 and El Paso at about 287.5bps in July 2026. The yield gap of about 95bps overstates the spread gap of about 62bps because Treasury levels moved between the two dates. Make the relative cost claim on spreads. The "10 year" benchmark for 287.5bps is not confirmed, since sources say only "over Treasuries". Drop "10 year" or confirm it from Bloomberg.
7. **Exact El Paso figures (ids 29, 32).** Bloomberg derived press gives "about 7.5%" and "about $12.5bn". The exact 7.534% and $12.55bn appear only in Zhitong based aggregators (AiCoin, Bitget), and S&P's preliminary rating was assigned to $12.3bn of notes before the deal was upsized. Use "about 7.5%" and "about $12.5bn", or confirm the exact figures from Bloomberg or the offering.

Labelling dates helps, but a spread version is cleaner.
